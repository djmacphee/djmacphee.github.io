Founder & builderSquad Steward · 2026 · in alpha

A full web platform in eight weeks, built with AI coding agents

Event volunteers are still run on spreadsheets and email chains. Squad Steward is a volunteer coordination platform: people sign up for shifts, coordinators check them in, invite last year's volunteers back, import and export spreadsheets, and email everyone. The idea came from years of running volunteer teams myself, and it serves any event: a school sign-up, weekend snacks for a kids' team, a small community event or a large event with many teams.

I ran the whole build as an agentic engineering workflow. Claude Code wrote and changed the code; I set the direction, reviewed every change, and kept the agents honest with tests.

  1. Plans and specs live in Jira and Confluence; MCP connections let the agents read and update them directly.
  2. Agents implement against unit and end-to-end tests: Vitest for the domain, Playwright for real user flows.
  3. Every change goes through lint, type checks and the test suites before it deploys.

Squad Steward is in alpha. Want a preview? Email me.

Roku · 2026

A one-stop dashboard for Dev, QA and Product

Project status was spread across tools, and each group read it differently. I built a dashboard that brought our workflows into one place, so developers, QA and product saw the same state of a project, and stakeholders did too. It ran from a Docker image: a Next.js app in TypeScript, with Python and Bash scripts doing the work behind it.

It was also a safe place to learn AI. It brought the team's local skills together and gave people somewhere to try AI in their own work, where mistakes were allowed and wins were celebrated. I used it to show how scripts written with AI can replace expensive MCP calls for repetitive tasks, and what different AI models would cost for the same work.

Result: used by multiple teams, including all 23 people on my team, as one place for project status across Dev, QA and Product; more of the team using AI; and lower AI spend by moving repetitive work from MCPs to scripts.
Roku · 2024–2026

An AI triage agent for CI failures

Hundreds of automated tests on software for millions of devices meant a steady stream of failures, and small teams were losing hours sorting real bugs from noise. I created an AI triage tool in 2024 that diagnoses errors and points to the cause, then grew it into a skills-based agent connected to our development systems through Model Context Protocol.

Result: engineers spent their time on product goals instead of triage, and root-cause analysis got faster.

How I work

Approach

01

Automate the feedback, not just the tests

A test is only useful if someone acts on its result quickly. I build pipelines where results arrive daily, failures are triaged, and production incidents feed back into the suite.

02

Put AI where the toil is

Triage, release checks and test generation are where AI agents earn their keep. I connect models to real systems through MCP, measure them against each other, and keep humans on the decisions.

03

Small teams, clear standards

Remote teams do well with a steady rhythm, written standards and shared ownership of quality. My job is to clear the path so engineers can do the work they're good at.
